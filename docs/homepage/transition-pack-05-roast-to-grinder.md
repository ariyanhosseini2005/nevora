## Frame-by-frame transition logic

### Frames 472–483 — Finish the orbit

- Roast color, pores, crease depth, scar, and dry surface are already final.
- The hero completes its last clockwise arc inside the drum.
- Camera velocity begins changing from co-orbit to a controlled forward push.
- The S-shaped crease rotates toward the optical center without resetting.

### Frames 484–491 — Let the bean own the lens

- The camera approaches the hero surface on the crease axis.
- Bean scale grows smoothly until it covers at least 80% of the frame.
- Only thin copper drum slivers remain visible around the outer edges.
- No grinder tooth may be clearly readable before the hero owns the frame.

### Frames 492–499 — Transfer circular geometry

- The crease remains locked to the center while the camera crosses the scale boundary.
- Copper arcs at the periphery become cooler graphite-steel arcs behind the occlusion.
- Drum perforation rhythm becomes the concentric spacing of the burr ring.
- Warm upper-left bean highlight continues into one controlled steel edge highlight.

### Frames 500–515 — Reveal the burr threshold

- Opposing burr faces emerge symmetrically around the same optical axis.
- The whole bean remains in front of the contact channel and continues forward.
- Focus expands just enough to read the steel teeth without losing the hero pores.
- Contact, fracture, fragments, and grounds belong to the next pack and are forbidden here.

## Optical and material rules

- Lens language moves from 65 mm close macro to 100 mm extreme macro.
- Apparent scale growth is caused only by forward camera movement.
- The hero's final identity from Heat Pack 04 must remain intact.
- Steel burrs require real machined edges, tooth depth, micro-scratches, and restrained speculars.
- Metal color changes from copper warmth to graphite neutrality behind the bean occlusion.
- Burr symmetry is allowed, but the bean remains organically asymmetrical.

## Rejection conditions

- A cut or dissolve between unrelated roaster and grinder compositions.
- Grinder teeth visible before the bean owns most of the frame.
- A different, mirrored, smoothed, or redesigned hero bean.
- Fracture, cracks, fragments, grounds, sparks, or powder in anchor C.
- Chrome-like CGI metal, impossible burr geometry, or razor blades touching the lens.
- Scale jump, rotation reset, flipped light, or changed optical axis.
