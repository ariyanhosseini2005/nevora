## Locked glass identity

- One small, low cylindrical double-wall espresso glass.
- Simple slightly outward rim and thick optical base.
- No handle, logo, pattern, decoration, or vessel swap.
- Proportions, wall thickness, center axis, and reflections remain consistent from frame 664 onward.
- Glass scale changes only because the camera pulls back.

## Frame-by-frame transition logic

### Frames 624–655 — Stabilize and follow the stream

- Smaller extraction threads merge into one primary central stream.
- Camera travels downward beside the same liquid line.
- Viscosity, amber edge transmission, and micro-bubbles remain readable.
- No glass, rim, table, or final environment is visible before frame 656.

### Frames 656–671 — Introduce the glass from below

- The glass rim rises gradually from the lower frame as the camera follows the stream.
- The rim remains centered beneath the unbroken liquid line.
- Optical refraction appears before the complete vessel silhouette.
- Portafilter remains partially visible above to preserve spatial depth.

### Frames 672–687 — Impact and begin the only pullback

- Stream reaches the center of the glass and creates one restrained impact dimple.
- Crema expands outward from the impact point in a natural circular bloom.
- Lens language begins opening from 100 mm macro toward 50 mm.
- Camera reveals the rim and upper glass without changing the liquid axis.

### Frames 688–707 — Reveal, taper, and decelerate

- The full double-wall glass and warm wooden surface become visible.
- Portafilter moves naturally beyond the upper edge.
- Stream width decreases monotonically into a delicate thread.
- Crema develops fine bubbles and a gentle organic swirl, never a designed pattern.

### Frames 708–719 — Stop and settle

- The final liquid thread disconnects under real surface tension.
- Crema motion damps into a quiet golden layer.
- Focus expands to the full glass and immediate surface.
- Camera reaches exactly zero velocity only at frame 719.
- No logo or copy appears inside the transformation film.

## Post-film brand stillness gate

- Frames 720–743 are a separate 24-frame stillness hold for future UI/brand reveal.
- Camera, glass, table, and background remain locked.
- Only sub-pixel crema settling and natural film grain may continue.
- Brand opacity may begin only after the hold is proven calm in forward and reverse scroll.
- This hold is outside the 720-frame transformation film and must not alter its ending.

## Optical and liquid rules

- Lens language opens continuously from 100 mm extreme macro to 50 mm during the pullback.
- Stream center may drift no more than 2% of frame width before tapering.
- Glass refraction must distort the liquid and background physically.
- Espresso stays translucent at thin amber edges and nearly opaque at its deep center.
- Crema uses small natural bubbles, tonal variation, and a soft edge; no latte art.
- Upper-left light, exposure, and background warmth remain constant.

## Rejection conditions

- Glass appearing suddenly, changing design, scale-jumping, or floating.
- Stream breaking before frame 708 or moving away from the glass center.
- Unrelated pullback composition, abrupt lens jump, or portafilter teleport.
- Excessive splash, chaotic crema, latte art, decorative steam, or syrup-like liquid.
- Saucer, spoon, loose coffee beans, second glass, hand, person, logo, or text.
- Camera movement or focus breathing after the final stillness point.
