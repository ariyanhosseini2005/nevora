## Frame-by-frame transition logic

### Frames 204–215 — Close the physical aperture

- Camera acceleration continues from the falling cherry.
- Basket fibers expand from the outer frame toward the optical center.
- Hero cherry remains on the central-lower axis until at least frame 215.
- Motion blur belongs to the near-lens weave, not the hero cherry.

### Frames 216–227 — Own the lens

- Fiber texture covers 95–100% of the image.
- The environment change is allowed only after full physical coverage.
- One narrow upper-left highlight remains visible and travels downward-right.
- No water, seed, or processing equipment is visible before frame 228.

### Frames 228–233 — Change the medium while hidden

- The warm fiber highlight stretches and begins to refract.
- Near-black woven texture loses contrast and becomes a dark water surface.
- Camera direction, velocity, and optical center remain unchanged.
- The hero object is still hidden; this prevents a visible teleport.

### Frames 234–244 — Reveal the same hero

- Fine bubbles and suspended particles establish underwater depth first.
- The crimson cherry re-enters on the central-lower axis.
- Its natural seam begins to open; the pale green seed is only partially visible.
- The reveal must feel caused by water movement, never by a dissolve.

## Optical and material rules

- Lens language: 85 mm at anchor A, moving toward 100 mm macro by anchor C.
- Focus distance must breathe through the occlusion instead of snapping.
- Key light remains upper-left; water changes its shape, not its source.
- Basket fibers need real fraying and irregular handmade spacing.
- Water needs physically plausible refraction, small bubbles, and restrained caustics.
- The cherry must preserve the same scar, asymmetry, scale evolution, and downward rotation.

## Rejection conditions

- A black frame with no readable physical fiber texture.
- A conventional fade-to-black or cross-dissolve.
- A different cherry, duplicated cherry, or fully separated seed at the reveal.
- Reversed light direction or a new camera axis.
- A splash explosion, magical portal, or surreal transformation.
- Water visible before full lens coverage.
