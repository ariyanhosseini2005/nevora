## Frame-by-frame transition logic

### Frames 512–523 — Load the crease

- Burr contact begins symmetrically on the outer lobes.
- Compression travels toward the existing S-shaped crease.
- One restrained stress line becomes visible; the bean remains at least 90% intact.
- Burr rotation and bean motion agree mechanically; no free-floating contact.

### Frames 524–535 — Preserve identity through fracture

- The natural crease becomes the primary separation path.
- The hero breaks first into 4–6 large pieces, not instant powder.
- Several pieces retain original porous skin, scar, tip, or crease geometry.
- Medium fragments may appear only after the primary pieces separate.

### Frames 536–543 — Let particles own the lens

- Large fragments move past the outer lens with controlled near-field blur.
- The camera advances into the central void created by the fracture.
- Smaller granules and realistic espresso grounds occupy the clear central path.
- Burr geometry may disappear only after fragments cover the environment handoff.

### Frames 544–560 — Bend forward motion into gravity

- Grinder and chute geometry curve the particle vector from forward to downward.
- The portafilter rim rises gradually from the lower frame.
- Concentric grinder geometry becomes the circular steel basket rim.
- Grounds are still falling; the coffee bed is uneven and incomplete.

## Particle distribution rules

- Grounds must contain plausible espresso-scale variation, never identical spheres.
- Large recognizable fragments reduce monotonically after frame 532.
- Fine particles remain subordinate and may not form an opaque dust cloud.
- Directional blur appears only on moving near-lens fragments.
- Particle density increases toward the center of the chute, then spreads slightly over the basket.
- Reverse scrubbing must recombine particles along the same physical paths.

## Optical and material rules

- Lens language remains around 100 mm macro throughout the scale transition.
- Camera stays on the original bean/crease axis until gravity owns the path.
- Upper-left key light remains constant across burrs, particles, and portafilter rim.
- Burr teeth need real thickness, sharp machined edges, and micro-scratches.
- Fragment interiors look porous and dry, not glassy or moist.
- Portafilter steel is brushed and restrained; it must not become mirror chrome.

## Rejection conditions

- Explosive radial shattering, sparks, smoke, or cinematic debris blast.
- Glass-like shards, uniform sand, flour-like dust, or identical particles.
- Grounds appearing instantly inside a finished flat bed.
- Portafilter visible before the particle stream bends downward.
- Burrs disappearing before fragments physically cover the lens.
- Duplicate whole beans, unrelated fragments, direction jump, or light flip.
